About GE HealthCare

GE HealthCare describes itself as a leading global medical technology and digital solutions innovator. Its stated purpose is to create a world where healthcare has no limits. The company makes imaging, ultrasound, patient care and diagnostic products used in hospitals around the world.

For a fresher, a regulated medical device manufacturer is a useful place to start. You learn how documentation, traceability, calibration and quality systems work when a product affects patient safety. These habits carry over to almost any engineering career.

Role Overview

The Graduate Engineer Trainee supports manufacturing and quality activities for Ultrasound and PCS products. The posting says the role gives hands-on exposure to the following areas:

  • Production operations and manufacturing processes
  • Equipment calibration and validation
  • Quality issue resolution
  • Manufacturing documentation
  • Supplier quality coordination
  • Continuous improvement initiatives

This is a manufacturing and quality engineering role, not a software job.

Key Responsibilities

The posting lists many duties. They fall into a few groups.

  • Production support: help with day-to-day manufacturing so that operations stay safe, compliant and efficient. Support installation and maintenance of manufacturing equipment. Help identify and order the tools and equipment needed.
  • Quality and issue resolution: support investigation of manufacturing and product quality issues on the line. Review and clear non-conforming material reports. Maintain the designated non-conforming product area.
  • Calibration, verification and validation: make sure calibration is completed on schedule and keep the records and reports. Perform verification and validation of equipment and processes, and help prepare the reports.
  • Supplier coordination: work with cross-functional teams and Supplier Quality Engineering on rejected material. Coordinate with suppliers and production on part rejections and rework.
  • Documentation: prepare and maintain work instructions with revision control. Maintain Device History Records. Support engineering change communication and controlled distribution of documents.
  • Training and improvement: train team members on changes to work instructions. Run Poka-Yoke projects using jigs and fixtures. Support Kaizen and Lean Action Work Outs.
  • Compliance: follow the site Quality Management System, Environmental, Health and Safety policies, and HR and administrative policies. Support assigned EHS activities and maintain capital goods inventory with proper identification and traceability.

Eligibility Criteria

  • B.E./B.Tech in Electrical Engineering, Electronics Engineering, Biomedical or Mechanical Engineering.
  • Fresh graduate, or up to one year of relevant experience.
  • Basic understanding of engineering principles, with an interest in manufacturing, production and quality.
  • Ability to understand and follow technical procedures, work instructions and quality requirements.
  • Good written and verbal communication skills.
  • Ability to work with manufacturing and cross-functional teams.

The posting does not state a minimum percentage, a passing-out year or an age limit. Check the LinkedIn page for any update before you apply.

Desired Skills (Good to Have)

These are preferred, not mandatory. They can set your application apart from other fresh graduates.

  • Academic project, internship or practical exposure to manufacturing, production or quality.
  • Basic knowledge of manufacturing processes, equipment calibration, verification and validation.
  • Awareness of quality concepts and structured problem-solving methods.
  • Basic knowledge of Lean concepts such as Kaizen, Poka-Yoke and 5S.
  • Exposure to technical documentation, work instructions or manufacturing records.
  • Strong attention to detail and accurate record keeping.
  • Willingness to learn in a regulated medical device manufacturing environment.

Use your project work

If you did a mini project, a lab calibration exercise or a workshop internship, describe it in manufacturing terms: the process, what could go wrong, how you checked it and how you documented it. Recruiters for this role look for process thinking and attention to detail.

Salary and Benefits

The posting does not disclose salary or stipend. GE HealthCare says only that its salary and benefits reflect an organisation of global strength and scale. Do not trust any figure from a third party. You can ask about compensation during the interview process.

The posting also says relocation assistance is not provided. If you live outside Bengaluru, plan for your own travel and accommodation.

Selection Process

The posting does not describe the selection stages. For a fresher manufacturing role at a company like this, candidates can reasonably expect resume screening followed by one or more interviews. These are not confirmed steps, so treat them as a preparation guide only. Interviews for such roles usually test your core engineering basics, project understanding, communication and attitude towards quality and safety.

How to Apply

  1. Update your resume so it is clearly aimed at manufacturing and quality. Keep it to one page and put your degree branch, projects and any internship at the top.
  2. Open the LinkedIn job posting through the link in the Official Links section below. Log in to your LinkedIn account.
  3. Check that the job still shows as accepting applications, then click Easy Apply.
  4. Fill in your contact details, upload your resume and answer the screening questions honestly.
  5. Review everything and submit. Save a screenshot of the confirmation.
  6. Keep your phone and email active. Check your spam folder for replies.

Apply early

No closing date is given. LinkedIn postings can close without notice once enough applications arrive, so apply as soon as your resume is ready.

Preparation Plan

You have a few days, so spend them on the topics this role actually uses.

  • Lean basics: learn 5S, Kaizen, Poka-Yoke and the seven wastes. Prepare one simple example of each, such as a jig that prevents a wrong part from being fitted.
  • Quality basics: understand non-conformance, root cause analysis (5 Why, fishbone), corrective and preventive action, and why records matter in regulated industries.
  • Calibration and measurement: revise instruments, accuracy and tolerance, traceability, and the difference between verification and validation.
  • Your branch fundamentals: electronics and electrical candidates should revise circuits, power supplies, sensors and PCB basics. Mechanical candidates should revise fits and tolerances, materials and drawings. Biomedical candidates should revise medical instrumentation basics.
  • Documentation: understand what a work instruction, revision control and a device history record are, even at a basic level.

Likely Interview Topics

  • Walk me through your final-year project and your role in it.
  • What is the difference between verification and validation?
  • What is Poka-Yoke? Give an example.
  • How would you handle a batch of rejected parts from a supplier?
  • Why is documentation important in medical device manufacturing?
  • Why manufacturing and quality instead of design or software?

Answer with simple, honest examples. If you do not know something, say how you would find out.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Sending a generic software-style resume. Rewrite it for a manufacturing and quality role.
  • Applying from the wrong branch. The listing names Electrical, Electronics, Biomedical and Mechanical.
  • Paying anyone for this job. GE HealthCare states it never asks for payment to process documents and never refers you to a third party to process applications.
  • Skipping the screening questions or giving inconsistent answers.
  • Ignoring that the role is on-site in a plant environment, with attention to safety and compliance.

Beware of fake offers

Never send money to anyone who claims they can get you a GE HealthCare job. Apply only through the official LinkedIn posting.
