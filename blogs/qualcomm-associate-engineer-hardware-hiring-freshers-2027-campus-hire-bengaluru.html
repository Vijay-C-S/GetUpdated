About Qualcomm

Qualcomm describes itself as a global leader in mobile technology for more than three decades. Its work spans industries such as automotive, health care, smart cities and robotics. The company says its 27,000+ employees include many who joined right out of college, working on wireless technology.

For a fresher, that matters. Campus hires at Qualcomm are expected to work on real products, not only practice projects. The posting says the work directly influences the various subsystems within the SoC (system on chip). That is the kind of exposure many VLSI and embedded freshers look for early in their career.

Role Overview

The posting looks for an "energetic, creative and self-driven engineer" to work in modem, multimedia, connectivity, computer vision and image processing, software implementation and hardware acceleration. The ideal candidate has strong problem-solving and analytical skills, along with creativity and a passion for innovation.

In practice, the posting says you will:

  • Perform detailed technical analysis of problems.
  • Translate ideas into models, software and/or hardware.
  • Work closely with other teams to help deliver real products.

The posting also notes that complex wireless devices are only as powerful as the software that runs them. So even hardware-leaning candidates should be comfortable with programming and scripting.

Hardware Areas Covered

The posting lists the following domains under this campus hire. You will likely be matched to one based on your background and interview performance.

  • SoC and hard macro physical design
  • SoC validation and debug
  • RF and analog layout
  • RF/analog/mixed signal/power IC design
  • Low power design
  • Board and FPGA design
  • Digital ASIC design
  • Design/SoC verification
  • CAD solution engineer
  • Design for Test (DFT)
  • CPU design

The posting does not say how many seats each domain has or how candidates are allocated. Read the list against your own projects and coursework, then mention the most relevant areas in your application.

Eligibility

Based on the posting, you should meet the following:

  • Hold a degree in Computer Science, Electrical/Electronic Engineering, Engineering or a related field. The minimum stated is an Associate's degree, but a Bachelor's degree is preferred.
  • Have an educational background in one or more of these: verifying SoCs with embedded RISC/DSP processors, communications or networking ASICs, Verilog or VHDL, C/C++, and Tcl/Perl/shell scripting.
  • Have RTL design experience and/or strong object-oriented programming knowledge.
  • For RF/Analog/Mixed Signal IC Design, know PLL, LNA, OpAmp, CMOS, ADC/DAC, Cadence, SpectreRF or layout.
  • Show excellent analytical and problem-solving skills.
  • Work well in teams and communicate clearly, both verbally and in writing.

Knowledge of wireless or wired communications and protocols, or of graphics, video and multimedia, is listed as a plus.

How to Apply

Preparation Plan

A short, focused plan works better than studying everything. Pick the domain closest to your strengths and prepare it well.

Core subjects

  • Digital design: number systems, combinational and sequential logic, FSMs, timing, setup and hold, and clock domain basics.
  • Verilog/VHDL: write small RTL blocks such as counters, FIFOs, and simple FSMs, and be ready to explain them.
  • C/C++ and scripting: pointers, OOP basics and data structures, plus basic Tcl, Perl or shell scripting.
  • Computer architecture: pipelining, caches and processor basics, useful for CPU design and verification roles.
  • Analog/RF (if applicable): CMOS basics, OpAmp, PLL, LNA, ADC/DAC and layout concepts.

Week-by-week approach

  1. Revise fundamentals and make short notes.
  2. Practise RTL coding and simulation on a free simulator.
  3. Solve coding problems in C/C++ for logic and speed.
  4. Prepare to discuss every project on your resume in depth.
  5. Do mock interviews with friends or seniors.

Interview Topics to Revise

Since the posting emphasises analysis and problem solving, expect questions that test how you think, not only what you remember.

  • Explain a project from your resume end to end: the problem, your part and the result.
  • Debugging scenarios, such as why a simulation fails or a signal is stuck.
  • Basics of low power techniques, DFT concepts such as scan, and physical design flow terms.
  • Verification ideas such as testbenches, assertions and coverage.
  • OOP and programming questions in C/C++.
  • Short HR questions about teamwork, communication and why you want this role.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying without reading the domains. Know which areas interest you and prepare for them.
  • A cluttered resume. Show only skills you can explain confidently.
  • Ignoring programming. Even hardware roles here expect C/C++ and scripting.
  • Waiting too long. The last date is not disclosed, and campus postings can close without notice.
  • Using unofficial links. Apply only via Qualcomm's own posting.
