Amazon Hiring Software Dev Engineer I (SDE I) 2026 Graduates: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai

Amazon Software Dev Engineer I 2026

Amazon is hiring Software Dev Engineer I (SDE I) through its University Talent Acquisition program for graduates of the 2026 batch. The listing (Job ID 10454438) shows openings in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai. It is one of the most sought-after entry-level software roles in India.

The basic requirement is simple: a bachelor's degree or above in computer science, computer engineering or a related field, and graduation in 2026. Previous internships are only preferred, not mandatory, so a strong fresher profile with solid fundamentals has a real chance.

Amazon states that applications are reviewed on a rolling basis, and no closing date is given. Since it receives a high volume of applicants, applying early and keeping your profile sharp matters.

About Amazon and the SDE Role

Amazon describes itself as a company where customer obsession is part of its DNA. Its Software Development Engineers use modern technology to solve complex problems and see the impact of their work first-hand. The listing says the problems SDEs tackle influence millions of customers, sellers and products around the world.

For a fresher, this means early ownership. Amazon looks for people who enjoy creating new products, features and services from scratch while handling ambiguity. The listing also mentions a fast pace, where development cycles are measured in weeks, not years. If you like building things and learning quickly, that environment can accelerate your growth.

Role Overview and Key Responsibilities

The listing lists these key responsibilities for the SDE I role:

  • Collaborate with experienced cross-disciplinary Amazonians to conceive, design and bring innovative products and services to market.
  • Design and build innovative technologies in a large distributed computing environment and help lead fundamental changes in the industry.
  • Create solutions to run predictions on distributed systems with exposure to innovative technologies at incredible scale and speed.
  • Build distributed storage, index and query systems that are scalable, fault-tolerant, low cost and easy to manage and use.
  • Design and code the right solutions starting with broadly defined problems.
  • Work in an agile environment to deliver high-quality software.

Notice the repeated theme of distributed systems, scale and fault tolerance. Even if you have not built such systems, understanding the basic ideas will help you in interviews and on the job.

Eligibility Criteria

Basic qualifications

  • Bachelor's degree or above in computer science, computer engineering or a related field.
  • Students who have graduated in 2026.

Preferred qualifications

  • Previous technical internship(s).
  • Experience with distributed, multi-tiered systems, algorithms and relational databases.
  • Experience in optimization mathematics such as linear programming and nonlinear optimization.
  • Ability to articulate technical challenges and solutions clearly.
  • Comfort with ambiguous or undefined problems and the ability to think abstractly.

Preferred items are a bonus, not a gate. If you lack an internship, show your strength through projects, competitive programming, open-source contributions or well-documented college work.

Check your branch

The listing says "computer science, computer engineering, or a related field". If your degree is in ECE, EEE or another branch, check carefully whether you fit "related field" and be ready to show strong programming and CS fundamentals. The final call rests with Amazon's recruiters.

Locations and Salary

The listing shows three locations: Bengaluru (Karnataka), Hyderabad (Telangana) and Chennai (Tamil Nadu). The listing is under the ADCI - Karnataka entity. It does not say how location is allotted, so mention any preference politely if the application form or recruiter asks.

Salary and benefits are not disclosed in the listing. Do not trust random figures on forums or social media. Amazon's careers site has a Benefits section, and compensation details are normally discussed by the recruiter during the process.

Selection Process

The listing does not describe the rounds, so nothing official can be stated here. Amazon's careers site has a "How We Hire" page, which is the right place to read about its process and what to expect. The listing also mentions a candidate portal where you can log in to confirm your application was submitted and to check updates.

Since Amazon notes it works with a high volume of applicants, expect to wait. Check your email, including spam, and the portal regularly.

Accommodations

If you have a disability and need an adjustment during the application, interview or onboarding process, the listing points to Amazon's accommodations page under How We Hire.

How to Apply

  1. Open the official job page using the link in the Official Links section below.
  2. Read the full description and confirm you meet the basic qualifications, especially the 2026 graduation requirement.
  3. Click the Apply now button on the page.
  4. Sign in or create an account on the Amazon jobs portal.
  5. Fill in your personal, education and project details accurately.
  6. Upload an updated resume (see tips below).
  7. Submit, then log in to your candidate portal to confirm your application went through.

Apply only through the official amazon.jobs page. Do not pay money to anyone who promises a job at Amazon.

Resume Tips for Freshers

Keep your resume to one page. Lead with education, then projects, internships, skills and achievements. For every project, state what you built, which technologies you used and what the result was. Numbers help, but only use true ones.

Show the topics the listing cares about: algorithms, databases, distributed or multi-tiered systems. A project using a relational database with a backend service, or something that handles many users, is more relevant than a simple static website.

Preparation Plan

Because the process is not detailed in the listing, prepare broadly on fundamentals.

Weeks 1-2: Core data structures and algorithms

  • Arrays, strings, linked lists, stacks, queues, hash maps, trees, graphs, heaps.
  • Sorting, searching, recursion, two pointers, sliding window, binary search.
  • Practise daily on a coding platform and explain your approach aloud.

Weeks 3-4: Deeper problem solving

  • Dynamic programming, graph traversal, backtracking, greedy methods.
  • Time and space complexity analysis for every solution.
  • Edge cases and clean, readable code.

Weeks 5-6: CS fundamentals and design basics

  • Databases: SQL queries, joins, indexing, normalisation, transactions.
  • Operating systems, networking basics, object-oriented design.
  • Introductory ideas in distributed systems: scalability, replication, fault tolerance, caching, consistency.

Final stretch: Communication and mock practice

  • Practise explaining your thought process step by step.
  • Do mock interviews with friends or seniors.
  • Review Amazon's Leadership Principles on its careers site and prepare honest examples from college projects, teamwork and failures.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Missing the rolling review: applying late may reduce your chances because applications are reviewed as they arrive.
  • Ignoring eligibility: the listing is for 2026 graduates, so check your graduation year before applying.
  • Listing skills you cannot explain: anything on your resume can be asked about.
  • Memorising solutions without understanding them: interviewers usually change the problem slightly.
  • Staying silent while coding: explain your reasoning and trade-offs.
  • Trusting unofficial salary or process claims: stick to official Amazon pages.
