About IBM in India

IBM is a global technology and consulting company. Its work covers hybrid cloud, artificial intelligence, consulting, software, infrastructure and automation. In India, IBM has offices in several major cities, including Bengaluru, and works with banks, telecom companies, government bodies and large enterprises.

Most Indian freshers meet IBM through three kinds of roles. Software and engineering roles involve development, testing and platform work. Consulting and delivery roles involve application support, project work and client engagements. Business and operations roles cover areas such as HR, finance and sales support. The listing for Job ID 142095 will tell you which of these it belongs to.

IBM has a reputation for structured onboarding, internal training and a wide range of technologies. That makes it a good starting point if you want broad exposure early in your career.

Role Overview: What to Check on the Listing

The IBM job page follows a standard layout. Read each part carefully:

  • Introduction: a short summary of the team and its work.
  • Your role and responsibilities: the day-to-day tasks. This shows whether the job is development, support, consulting or something else.
  • Required education: the degree IBM expects, such as B.E/B.Tech, MCA, B.Sc or any graduate degree.
  • Required technical and professional expertise: the skills you must have, such as a programming language, cloud basics or database knowledge.
  • Preferred technical and professional expertise: nice-to-have skills that can improve your chances.
  • Required experience: this decides whether the job suits a fresher. IBM sometimes lists "0-1 year" or "no experience required", and sometimes asks for several years.

Do not apply just because the company name is attractive. If the listing asks for several years of experience, it is not a fresher role, and applying to it wastes your attempt.

Eligibility Checklist

Because we could not read the listing, use this checklist against the official text:

  • Your degree and branch match the "Required education" line.
  • Your passing-out year fits any batch or experience rule mentioned.
  • You have the must-have technical skills, not only the preferred ones.
  • You can work in the stated city or location, or the listing allows remote or hybrid work.
  • You meet any other conditions, such as notice period, backlog rules or availability for shifts.

If the listing does not say fresher or entry level, read the experience line closely. If it asks for prior work experience as a condition, it is not meant for freshers. Look for a different IBM opening in that case.

Selection Process (Typical for IBM)

The exact steps for Job ID 142095 are not stated on the pages we could access. IBM hiring for India generally includes some of these stages:

  1. Resume screening: recruiters match your profile to the required skills.
  2. Online assessment: this may cover aptitude, logical reasoning, coding or role-specific questions, depending on the job.
  3. Technical interview: questions on your projects, core subjects and the skills you listed.
  4. Behavioural or HR round: communication, teamwork, flexibility and your reasons for choosing IBM.

Not every role has every stage. Some experienced hires go straight to interviews. Expect the recruiter to tell you the process by email.

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week plan works better than last-minute cramming.

Week 1: Basics and Resume

  • Rewrite your resume to one page. Put skills and projects that match the listing first.
  • Revise core subjects for your branch. For IT and CSE roles, that means data structures, OOPS, DBMS, operating systems and networks.
  • Practise aptitude and logical reasoning for 30-45 minutes a day.

Week 2: Practice and Mock Interviews

  • Solve easy and medium coding problems in one language you know well.
  • Revise your projects. Be ready to explain what you built, which tools you used and what went wrong.
  • Learn basic cloud, AI and Agile terms. IBM works heavily in these areas.
  • Do a mock interview with a friend and practise a short, honest self-introduction.

Common Interview Topics

  • Self-introduction and why you want to join IBM.
  • Your final-year project, internship or training work.
  • A programming language you listed, including basic syntax and concepts.
  • SQL queries and database basics, if the role involves data.
  • Basic cloud ideas such as what cloud computing is and why companies use it.
  • Situational questions on teamwork, deadlines and handling feedback.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying without reading the experience requirement.
  • Listing skills you cannot explain in an interview.
  • Using different names, dates or marks on your resume and your documents.
  • Applying through unofficial links or paying anyone for a "referral" or "confirmed offer". IBM does not charge candidates for hiring.
  • Ignoring emails. Assessment links often expire within a few days.
  • Submitting a generic resume instead of tailoring it to the listing.

Beware of fake IBM offers

Scammers often use big company names to ask for registration or training fees. Apply only on the IBM careers portal and ignore anyone who asks for money.
