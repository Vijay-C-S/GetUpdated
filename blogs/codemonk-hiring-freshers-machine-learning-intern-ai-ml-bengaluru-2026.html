Codemonk Machine Learning Intern (AI/ML) opening in Bengaluru

Codemonk is hiring a Machine Learning Intern (AI/ML) for its Bengaluru office, and the role is aimed squarely at freshers. The opening is listed on Codemonk's own careers page as an on-site, entry-level internship with an experience range of 0-1 years. The work centres on generative AI, LLM APIs, RAG pipelines and deep learning.

If you have been building small projects with Python, OpenAI or Gemini APIs, FAISS or Hugging Face, this is a rare chance to turn them into real experience. The listing is open now, and no closing date is published on the official page, so apply early.

This guide covers what the role involves, the skills Codemonk asks for, the hiring process the company describes, and a practical plan to prepare for the take-home assignment and technical rounds.

About Codemonk

Codemonk describes itself as a fast-growing product engineering and IT services firm that helps clients build scalable digital solutions, from startups to large enterprises. Its LinkedIn company description mentions work for brands such as Unilever, Tata Power and greytHR.

For a fresher, a services and product engineering company is a good learning ground. You are exposed to different client problems, which means you pick up varied tools quickly instead of working on a single narrow product.

Role Overview

The intern will help build and optimise generative AI models and applications. The official listing splits the work into two broad areas.

Generative AI:

  • Design, implement and debug machine learning algorithms, with a focus on transformer-based models.
  • Build applications using LLM APIs from OpenAI, Anthropic Claude, Gemini and other providers.
  • Construct and fine-tune Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems using vector databases such as FAISS or Pinecone.
  • Learn to deploy AI applications into production with monitoring and optimisation.

Deep learning and computer vision:

  • Train and fine-tune deep learning models, including adapting pretrained models to new tasks.
  • Run and track experiments to compare models, parameters and datasets.
  • Prepare, clean and audit datasets for quality and relevance.
  • Evaluate models with suitable metrics and find where and why they underperform.
  • Get basic exposure to model optimisation and deployment.

This is a hands-on build role, not a pure research or data-labelling internship.

Eligibility and Skills Required

The official page gives only the experience band (0-1 years) and does not name a degree or graduation year. In practice, a B.E/B.Tech, BCA, MCA or M.Sc student or recent graduate in CS, IT, ECE or data science with relevant projects is a natural fit. Treat degree-specific preferences as unconfirmed.

  • Strong foundation in Python.
  • Familiarity with LLM APIs such as OpenAI GPT-4, Anthropic Claude or Cohere, and interest in building AI applications.
  • Understanding of deep learning fundamentals: transformers, attention mechanisms and how they are optimised.
  • Basic knowledge of embedding models (OpenAI, sentence-transformers) and vector databases.
  • Good problem-solving, debugging and analytical skills.
  • Plus point: experience with LangChain, LlamaIndex or Hugging Face libraries.

Stipend and Benefits

The official Codemonk page does not list a stipend and says compensation is discussed at the offer stage based on experience and scope. Listings on LinkedIn report a base range of ₹13,000-₹15,000 per month, and describe the role as a paid internship. Treat that figure as indicative, not confirmed.

The official page does list benefits: an annual learning budget, flexible hours outside core collaboration time, and medical insurance from day one. Confirm with the recruiter whether these apply to interns during the internship.

Ask about duration early

The internship length is not published. Ask about it in the telephonic screening, along with stipend and the chance of a full-time conversion.

Selection Process

Codemonk's careers page describes the hiring process for this role as follows:

  1. A 20-30 minute telephonic screening.
  2. A take-home assignment of roughly 3-4 hours.
  3. Two technical rounds.
  4. A cultural round with leadership.

The company says most candidates hear back within two weeks of the final interview. The take-home assignment is where many freshers are filtered, so give it real time and polish.

  1. Open the official application link from the Official Links section below.
  2. Fill in your details and attach an up-to-date resume.
  3. Add links to your GitHub and any AI/ML projects.
  4. Submit the form and keep your phone reachable for the screening call.

Applications go directly to Codemonk's team. There is no fee mentioned anywhere in the process, so be careful of anyone asking you to pay.

Apply early

LinkedIn showed over 200 applicants within a day of posting. No deadline is published, so the role can close any time. Apply as soon as your resume is ready.

Preparation Plan

You do not need to be an expert, but you should be able to explain and demonstrate the basics. Here is a one-to-two-week plan.

  • Python: Revise lists, dicts, classes, file handling, virtual environments and writing clean functions. Be comfortable debugging with print statements and a debugger.
  • LLM APIs: Build a small chatbot that calls one provider, handles errors and keeps conversation history.
  • RAG: Build a mini RAG app: load PDFs, chunk text, create embeddings, store in FAISS, retrieve top results and answer with citations.
  • Deep learning: Understand backpropagation, overfitting, transformers and self-attention at a conceptual level. Fine-tune a small pretrained model with Hugging Face.
  • Evaluation: Know precision, recall, F1 and how to inspect failure cases rather than only quoting accuracy.

Make the take-home stand out

Write a short README with your approach, assumptions, results and what you would try next. A clear, reproducible notebook or repo beats a clever but messy one.

Likely Interview Topics

Based on the listed responsibilities, expect questions along these lines. These are preparation pointers, not an official question list.

  • What is attention, and why do transformers replace RNNs for many tasks?
  • How does a RAG pipeline work, and what affects retrieval quality (chunk size, embeddings, top-k)?
  • Difference between fine-tuning and prompt engineering, and when to use each.
  • How would you reduce hallucinations in an LLM application?
  • How do you audit a dataset for quality and bias before training?
  • Walk through one of your projects: what failed and how you fixed it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Listing tools you cannot explain. If LangChain is on your resume, be ready to explain what it does and when you would not use it.
  • Submitting a notebook with no explanation. Reviewers want to see your reasoning.
  • Ignoring evaluation. The role explicitly mentions metrics and finding where models underperform.
  • Skipping the cultural round preparation. Be ready to talk about teamwork, learning from feedback and why you want to work in Bengaluru on-site.

Also remember the role is on-site. If you are not in Bengaluru, plan your relocation and accommodation before accepting.
