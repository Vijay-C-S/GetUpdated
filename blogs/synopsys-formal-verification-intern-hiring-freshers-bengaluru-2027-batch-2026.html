About Synopsys

Synopsys describes its work as powering the "Era of Pervasive Intelligence". It creates technology behind high-performance silicon chips used in self-driving cars, smartwatches that monitor health, and renewable energy systems. The LinkedIn listing places the company in semiconductor manufacturing, software development and computer hardware.

For a fresher, this matters because chip design is a field where tools and methodology are as important as circuit knowledge. Working at a company that builds the verification and design tools gives you a view of how the industry actually works.

The posting says interns work on real-world projects with teams around the world, and it stresses values of integrity, excellence, leadership and passion.

Role Overview: What You Will Do

The posting lists three responsibilities:

  • Learn the latest formal verification methodology from experts and apply it to live projects.
  • Work with a team of seasoned FV engineers and execute validation as per defined plans.
  • Support quality improvements and contribute insights that help enhance verification tool capabilities.

Formal verification uses mathematical techniques to prove that a design meets its specification, rather than running simulations with test vectors. Properties are usually written as assertions, and a tool checks them exhaustively. Interns can expect to learn how to write and review properties, read counterexamples, and understand why a proof passes or fails.

Notice the third point: feedback on the tools themselves is part of the job. A curious intern who notices odd tool behaviour and reports it clearly can stand out.

Preparation Plan

If you have a few weeks, spend them on these areas, in order:

  • Digital design basics: combinational and sequential logic, FSMs, setup and hold, clock domain crossing, FIFOs, and metastability.
  • Computer architecture: pipelines, hazards, caches, memory hierarchy, and bus protocols such as AMBA basics.
  • Verilog/SystemVerilog: write small modules and testbenches. Know blocking versus non-blocking assignments and how to avoid latches.
  • SystemVerilog Assertions: learn immediate versus concurrent assertions, implication operators, and sequences such as |-> and ##n.
  • Formal concepts: safety versus liveness, assume/assert/cover, bounded versus unbounded proofs, and what a counterexample means.
  • Scripting: small Python or Tcl scripts to parse logs, and comfortable Linux shell use.

Build one small formal project

Take a simple FIFO or arbiter in Verilog, write a handful of SVA properties, and run them with any open-source formal tool such as SymbiYosys. Even a small, working project gives you something concrete to discuss in the interview.

Likely Interview Topics

Expect questions such as: What is the difference between simulation and formal verification? What is an assertion, and how do you write one for a FIFO that must never overflow? What is a counterexample? How do you handle state-space explosion? Be ready to explain every project line on your resume, and have a clear answer for why you want to work in verification instead of design.

Communication is listed as a requirement, so practise explaining a technical problem in simple words. Interviewers value clear reasoning over memorised definitions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying without any Verilog/SystemVerilog project on the resume.
  • Listing Python, Perl or Tcl without being able to write a short script on the spot.
  • Ignoring the graduation-year preference. If you graduate in another year, be honest and highlight your strengths.
  • Waiting for a deadline that was never published.
  • Using third-party sites as an application source instead of the official posting.

Beware of fake fees

Legitimate employers do not charge for internships. If anyone asks for money to "confirm" a Synopsys internship, ignore the request and report it.
