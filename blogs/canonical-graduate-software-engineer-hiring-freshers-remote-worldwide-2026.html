Canonical is hiring Graduate Software Engineers for remote Open Source and Linux roles

Canonical, the company behind Ubuntu, is hiring Graduate Software Engineers for its 2026 and 2027 graduate batches. The role is globally remote (home based), and the official Greenhouse posting says teams work across EMEA, APAC and AMER time zones.

This is one of the few entry-level openings where you work on the Linux stack itself, from boot loaders and kernels to containers and Kubernetes. The posting is clear that the process is highly competitive, so it suits freshers with strong academics, real projects and Linux experience.

Below you will find the role details, eligibility, a realistic preparation plan and the mistakes to avoid. Read the official posting once more before you apply, because the employer's page is the only source for final conditions.

About Canonical

Canonical is a global platform company that publishes Ubuntu, one of the most widely used Linux distributions across desktops, servers, clouds and devices. Its engineers work on both open source products and commercial business systems.

The company's teams are spread around the world and mostly work from home. Canonical says it tries to keep each team in a single time zone, so you always have colleagues alongside you to coach and mentor you.

Role Overview

The posting describes a very wide range of work. Teams use Python, Golang, Rust, C/C++, JavaScript and Bash. The work spans BIOS, boot loaders, firmware, kernel and drivers, up to desktop applications, containers and cloud. Devices range from a Raspberry Pi and new RISC-V boards to supercomputers and Kubernetes clusters.

Some teams package thousands of pieces of software, while others contribute deeply to one codebase. Canonical says it will work with you to find teams and projects that match your skills and motivation.

  • Shape your product roadmap at global sprints every six months.
  • Focus on design and user experience, even for developer tools and command line apps.
  • Write high quality, resilient and performant code that may serve millions of daily users.
  • Work towards mastery of key programming languages and Linux system knowledge.
  • Engage with users and the open source community through code reviews and issue trackers.
  • Respond to customer issues as a priority and learn enterprise requirements.
  • Build awareness of security in software design and implementation.

Eligibility Criteria

The official page lists these expectations:

  • Exceptional academic track record in both high school and university.
  • Undergraduate degree in Computer Science, Business Informatics, Mathematics or another STEM discipline with programming courses.
  • Confidence to deliver high quality code in one of Python, Rust, C/C++, Golang, JavaScript or Java.
  • Experience with Ubuntu or another Linux distribution.
  • Personal projects in technology beyond the curriculum.
  • Professional written and spoken English.
  • Ability to travel internationally twice a year for company events of up to two weeks.

Nice-to-have skills

Upstream open source contributions, packaging or distribution contributions (Debian, Fedora, Arch, Nix or similar), leadership and organisation skills, presentation and writing skills, and business or commercial interest.

The posting does not state a minimum percentage or CGPA, but it stresses a consistent high-performing academic record. Treat strong marks as an expectation, not a bonus.

Work Pattern and Travel

This is full time work, five days a week. Canonical says it does not generally expect weekends and nights, and it offers good holidays, but it expects productive and intense engagement Monday to Friday.

All engineering teams meet in person twice a year for a deep sprint. The company presents this as a way to learn about many technologies and visit new cities. Make sure you can hold a valid passport and travel internationally before you apply, since travel of up to two weeks is a stated requirement.

Salary and Benefits

The posting does not give a salary figure. It says Canonical considers geographical location, experience and performance when shaping compensation worldwide, and that it revisits compensation twice a year. The excerpt we read also mentions benefits in addition to base pay, but the full list was cut off, so check the official page for details.

Do not trust random salary numbers

Any figure shared on social media or third-party sites is unofficial. Ask about compensation for your country during the interview process.

Selection Process

The official page does not publish the stages in the text we read. It only says the process is highly competitive and needs effort and excellence. Expect a multi-step process that tests academics, coding ability, Linux knowledge and communication. Do not rely on unverified stage lists from the internet; follow the emails you receive from Canonical.

Since the whole team works remotely and in English, written communication is likely to matter from the first application form onward. Answer every question carefully and specifically.

How to Apply

  1. Open the official Greenhouse posting through the link in the Official Links section below.
  2. Read the full role description and confirm you meet the degree and travel requirements.
  3. Prepare your resume with academic results, projects, GitHub profile and any open source contributions.
  4. Fill in the application form with honest, detailed answers.
  5. Submit and keep a record of the confirmation email.
  6. Watch your inbox and spam folder for follow-ups.

Apply early

No closing date is shown on the posting, and such roles can close once enough candidates are found. Apply as soon as your profile is ready.

Preparation Plan

You do not need to be an expert, but you must show real Linux and coding depth. A practical plan for the next four to six weeks:

  • Use Linux daily. Install Ubuntu or another distribution, and get comfortable with the shell, systemd, package managers, permissions and networking basics.
  • Pick one language. Choose Python, Go, Rust or C and write small tools, such as a CLI utility or a log parser, with tests and a README.
  • Contribute upstream. Start with documentation fixes or small bugs in projects you use. Even one merged pull request makes your profile stand out.
  • Try packaging. Build a simple Debian package or read how Ubuntu packages are maintained.
  • Revise fundamentals. Data structures, algorithms, operating systems, networking and basic security concepts.

Public, working projects and open source contributions are your best proof for this role.

Topics to Revise

Because the work covers low-level and high-level layers, interviewers may explore both. Likely areas based on the role description include:

  • Linux boot process, kernel basics, processes, memory and file systems.
  • Git workflows, code reviews and issue tracking.
  • Containers, Kubernetes basics and cloud concepts.
  • Secure coding and cryptography fundamentals.
  • Your own projects: why you built them, what failed and what you learned.

Explain your decisions

Be ready to explain design choices in your projects clearly in English, both spoken and written.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying without any Linux experience or personal projects.
  • Submitting a generic resume that lists only coursework.
  • Ignoring the international travel requirement.
  • Copying answers from the internet or an AI tool without personalising them.
  • Assuming a fixed salary or process from unofficial sources.

Also note that the posting is open to global candidates, but it does not specifically confirm hiring in India or any country-specific rules. Check whether your location and work authorisation are supported when you apply.

Official Links and Final Checklist

  • Degree in CS, Maths or another STEM field with programming courses
  • Strong academic record from school and university
  • Linux experience and one core programming language
  • Personal projects and GitHub profile ready
  • Passport and ability to travel twice a year
  • Resume updated and proofread

Final Takeaway

Canonical's graduate role is a rare chance to start a career inside the open source and Linux world, working remotely with a global team. It is demanding, and the company itself calls the process highly competitive.

If you have strong grades, a Linux habit and projects you can show, apply through the official link in the Official Links section above and prepare thoroughly. Spend the time before applying on building proof of your skills, because that is what this role rewards.
