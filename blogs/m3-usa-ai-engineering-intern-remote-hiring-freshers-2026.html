About M3

M3 describes itself as a Japanese global leader providing technology and research solutions to the healthcare industry. The group operates across the US, Asia and Europe and serves over 5.8 million physician members worldwide through websites such as mdlinx.com, m3.com, research.m3.com, Doctors.net.uk, medigate.net and medlive.cn.

M3 Inc. is publicly traded on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (2413, part of the NIKKEI 225) and was ranked in Forbes' Global 2000 list in 2020. Its services cover market research, medical education, ethical drug promotion, clinical development, job recruitment and clinic appointment services. M3 has offices in Japan, the UK, France, Germany, Brazil, Sweden, China, the USA and South Korea, as well as India.

For a fresher, this matters because you would be working inside a large, listed group where your tools are used by real business teams rather than a toy startup demo. The official M3 USA careers site describes the company mission as changing the world of medicine through full use of the power of the Internet.

Role Overview

The company is hiring an AI Engineering Intern to join M3 USA. The title is "AI Engineering", but the description makes clear that this is a builder role. You will not just fine-tune models in a notebook; you will build small, useful software that someone in the business actually needs.

The posting groups the work into three themes: build and ship, work with the businesses, and AI-augmented development. Together they describe a person who can talk to users, turn vague requests into something buildable this week, and use AI tools with good judgment.

What You Will Do

Build and ship

  • Own a project end to end: understand the problem, build a working version, put it in front of users and improve it based on feedback.
  • Write code that other people can read and maintain. The posting stresses that AI-generated or not, the code carries your name.
  • Demo your work regularly to the product and engineering team, and to the business teams that requested it.

Work with the businesses

  • Talk to the people whose jobs your tool touches.
  • Watch how they actually work instead of taking a feature request at face value.
  • Translate what you hear into something you can build this week, not next quarter.

AI-augmented development

  • Use agentic AI tools such as Claude Code as your default workflow.
  • Develop judgment about where these tools help and where they quietly produce garbage.
  • Try new tools as they ship and report back to the engineering team, including the failures.

What this tells you

M3 wants honest feedback on AI tools, not hype. In your application and interview, talk about a time an AI agent gave you wrong or messy code and how you caught it.

Eligibility

Required

  • Actively pursuing, or recently obtained, a degree in Computer Science or a related field.
  • Hands-on use of at least one AI coding agent to build something. A class project, side project or hackathon entry counts.
  • Working knowledge of a programming language such as Python, TypeScript or similar.
  • Comfort in a terminal. The posting says you should not need a GUI for git.
  • Ability to pick up unfamiliar tools by reading docs and trying things.

Preferred

  • Web development experience: HTML, CSS, JavaScript and any modern framework.
  • Knowledge of how LLMs work under the hood, not only how to prompt them.
  • Anything you have built that other people used, even briefly.

Branches like CSE, IT, AI and ML, Data Science, and closely related ECE or EEE profiles with strong coding experience can reasonably fit "related field", but the final call rests with M3. No specific graduation year, percentage cut-off or backlog rule is stated in the posting.

Preparation Plan

You can use the next one or two weeks well if you prepare in a focused way.

Days 1-3: Build one proof project

Pick a small real problem, such as a tool that cleans a spreadsheet, summarises a document set or automates a repetitive report. Build it with an AI coding agent, deploy it somewhere, and write a short README describing the problem, your approach and what the AI got wrong.

Days 4-6: Sharpen fundamentals

  • Revise Python or TypeScript basics: data structures, functions, error handling, async code and packages.
  • Practise git from the command line: branch, commit, rebase, resolve conflicts and push.
  • Get comfortable in a Linux or macOS style terminal: navigation, environment variables, running scripts.

Days 7-9: Understand LLMs a little deeper

  • Learn what tokens, context windows, embeddings and temperature mean.
  • Understand tool calling and how agents loop between reasoning and actions.
  • Know why models hallucinate and how you can reduce the risk with tests, retrieval and review.

Days 10-12: Practise explaining

Record yourself demoing your project in three minutes to a non-technical person. This role involves regular demos to business teams, so clear explanation counts as much as clean code.

Interview Topics to Revise

  • Walk-through of your best project: problem, design choices, mistakes, what you would change.
  • How you use AI coding agents day to day, and where you stop trusting them.
  • Reading and reviewing code: how you check AI-generated output before committing it.
  • Basic web concepts: HTTP, REST APIs, JSON, front-end and back-end split.
  • Simple coding problems in Python or TypeScript.
  • How you would gather requirements from a non-technical user and break them into a one-week deliverable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Claiming AI skills without a project. The posting asks for something you have built, so an empty "AI enthusiast" resume will not stand out.
  • Pasting AI output you cannot explain. If asked about a line of code, you must be able to defend it.
  • Sending a generic resume that ignores terminal, git and Python or TypeScript.
  • Ignoring the business side. Mention any time you spoke to users or got feedback.
  • Skipping the location and work authorisation details on the form.
  • Applying with a broken GitHub link or a private repository.
